## Detailed notes

### sport_en_053 ⚠️

- **Question**: Why is a biathlon competitor penalised with a 150 m penalty loop for every shot they miss?
- **Marked correct**: "Roughly the distance a top skier covers in the time a missed shot would have saved"
- **Clarity concern**: This is a popular post-hoc explanation but the actual historical IBU rationale is iterative competitive-balance calibration (the loop was 150m in sprint/pursuit/mass-start formats specifically because shorter loops over-favoured shooting accuracy and longer loops over-penalised it). The stated reason is colloquially defensible but not the canonical explanation.
- **Suggested fix**: Soften phrasing to "Roughly calibrated so ski speed and shooting accuracy weigh equally on final time" — which matches both the fun_fact and the official rationale. Or keep as-is and treat as acceptable trivia-style reasoning.

### sport_en_057 ⚠️

- **Question**: What did the introduction of the offside rule change in early association football?
- **Marked correct**: "It ended the era of forwards permanently camped near the opponent's goal"
- **Clarity concern**: The original 1863 offside rule was rugby-like — virtually any player ahead of the ball was offside. The "forwards camped at goal" era was actually addressed by the 1866 three-defender rule, and the 1925 reform to two defenders (described in the fun_fact) caused the dramatic goal increase. Stem says "the introduction" without specifying which version.
- **Suggested fix**: Reword to "What did the 1925 offside-rule reform change in association football?" — aligns stem with the fun_fact's content.

### sport_en_062 ⚠️

- **Question**: Why is the marathon almost always the final event on the athletics programme at the Olympic Games?
- **Marked correct**: "Tradition since 1896 places it as the symbolic closing event of the Games"
- **Factual concern**: The 1896 Athens marathon was on day 5 of 9, not the closing event. The marathon-as-finale tradition is real but solidified gradually through the 20th century, not "since 1896."
- **Suggested fix**: Reword the correct answer to "Tradition gradually placed it as the symbolic closing event of the Games, echoing the legend of Pheidippides" — drop the 1896 origin claim.

### sport_en_072 ⚠️

- **Question**: In which sport would you legitimately attempt a 'Phelps' turn?
- **Marked correct**: Competitive swimming butterfly
- **Factual concern**: "Phelps turn" is not a recognised, named butterfly turn technique. It appears in informal commentary but is not codified in any swimming reference (FINA technique manuals, coaching texts). The fun_fact treats it as "slang" but the stem treats it as a real named manoeuvre. Could be confusing or unfair.
- **Suggested fix**: Either delete (ambiguity-based, can't be cleanly resolved without inventing a fact) OR rephrase as "Michael Phelps was famous for his especially fast turns in which stroke?" with butterfly correct.

### sport_en_094 ⚠️

- **Suggested fix**: Reword to "What is the function of the textured/perforated surface beneath the base grip of a tennis racquet?" — same correct answer, more accurate terminology.

### sport_en_098 🚨

The fun_fact itself names Guardiola and Messi as the 2010s revivers, contradicting the stem's "Brazilian" attribution. "Falso 9" is also Spanish/Portuguese, not specifically Brazilian Portuguese terminology.
- **Suggested fix**: Drop the "Brazilian" word from the stem — "Which footballing role did the 'false nine' famously revive in the 2010s?" — or delete.
