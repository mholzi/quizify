## Detailed notes

### tech_en_059

- **Question**: Why did IBM choose Microsoft's DOS over its main rival CP/M for the original IBM PC?
- **Marked correct**: CP/M's author missed the crucial meeting with IBM
- **Factual concern**: The "Kildall went flying instead of meeting IBM" story is one of the most famous bits of computing lore but is genuinely contested. Kildall always disputed the account. Multiple historians (notably Stephen Manes, Paul Carroll, and DRI insiders) point to the real deal-breaker being a mix of NDA-refusal (Dorothy Kildall, his wife and DRI's business manager, wouldn't sign IBM's NDA on the spot) plus less-friendly licensing terms vs Microsoft's per-machine fee model.
- **Mitigation**: The fun_fact already acknowledges "Historians dispute the details" — so the question is internally honest about it. Not a fix_needed; flagging as concern so Markus knows the marked-correct answer is the popular-myth version rather than the strict-historical version.
- **Suggested fix (optional)**: Could be rephrased to "What is the most widely-told story about why IBM picked DOS over CP/M?" to lean into the lore explicitly rather than presenting it as settled history. Or leave as-is — most trivia packs accept the popular version.

### tech_en_067

- **Question**: Why did early iPhone cameras lack a flash for the first three generations?
- **Marked correct**: Apple judged early LED flashes too ugly and too power-hungry
- **Fun-fact concern**: The factual hardware claim is correct — original iPhone (2007), iPhone 3G (2008) and iPhone 3GS (2009) had no flash; the iPhone 4 (2010) introduced one. The *reason* given ("too ugly and too power-hungry") is plausible Apple-style design reasoning but is not documented in any official Apple statement I can verify. The "ship no feature rather than a bad one" framing is Apple-folklore commentary, not a sourced quote.
- **Mitigation**: The factual hardware timeline is unambiguous, so as a quiz item it still has a defensible correct answer (the distractors are wrong: Nokia LED patents didn't block Apple, and "waiting for a flash thin enough" is also unsupported). The concern is just that the "right answer" rationale is editorial. Acceptable as-is for a trivia pack.
- **Suggested fix (optional)**: Rephrase question to "Which is the most commonly cited reason..." or rephrase fun_fact to drop the unverifiable "famously reluctant" attribution.

### tech_en_071

- **Question**: Why did 19th-century umbrella makers go to court when steel ribs replaced whalebone ribs?
- **Marked correct**: Whalebone-rib patents tried to block the cheaper steel ones
- **Factual concern**: The Samuel Fox / 1852 Paragon patent is solid history. Mid-19th-century umbrella-frame patent fights certainly happened (Fox was litigious about his own patents). But the specific framing — that *whalebone-rib patent-holders* sued to block *steel-rib patent-holders* — is harder to source. Most documented disputes were between competing steel-frame designs, not whalebone-vs-steel. The story may be a conflation.
- **Mitigation**: The two distractors are both factually wrong (steel ribs weren't declared dangerous in lightning storms; there were no "whaling unions" with that kind of supply contract), so a player working by elimination still lands on the correct option. The concern is whether the framing of the "correct" answer matches the historical record cleanly.
- **Suggested fix (optional)**: Could be rephrased to "Why was there legal wrangling around umbrella frames in the 19th century?" with the answer "Competing patents between materials and designs" — broader and easier to defend. Or leave as-is and treat as folk-history.
