## Detailed notes

### tech_en_011 (concern)

- **Question**: Why is a software glitch called a 'bug'?
- **Marked correct**: An actual moth was found inside an early computer
- **Factual concern**: The term "bug" for technical malfunction predates the famous 1947 Harvard Mark II moth incident — Thomas Edison used "bug" in this sense in letters from 1878. Grace Hopper's team's logbook note "first actual case of bug being found" is itself a wink at the term already being in common engineering use. The fun_fact is accurate about the moth, but the question premise ("why is it called a bug") is historically misleading.
- **Ambiguity-based**: Yes — the answer is the popular myth rather than the etymological truth. Cleanly resolvable by reframing the question.

### tech_en_049 (concern)

- **Question**: Why did some smart fridges in 2014 reportedly send spam emails?
- **Marked correct**: They were hijacked into a botnet via weak credentials
- **Factual concern**: Proofpoint's January 2014 report claimed compromised IoT devices including a fridge were part of a spam botnet. The claim was widely reported but security researchers (including Symantec) later cast doubt on whether a fridge specifically was confirmed. The question's "reportedly" hedges adequately for live-game use; included here for completeness.
- **Ambiguity-based**: No — the cautious wording covers the dispute. No fix needed.
