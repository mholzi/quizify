## Detailed notes

### music_en_009 🚨

- **Question**: "Which Russian composer orchestrated Mussorgsky's 'Pictures at an Exhibition' into its famous version?"
- **Marked correct**: Maurice Ravel
- **Factual / Clarity fail**: Ravel was French, not Russian. The question's own fun_fact contradicts the premise ("Ravel wasn't Russian at all"). Players who know Ravel was French will rule him out, and players who pick a Russian distractor (Rimsky-Korsakov is plausible) get marked wrong.
- **Suggested fix**: drop the word "Russian" — "Which composer orchestrated Mussorgsky's 'Pictures at an Exhibition' into its famous version?"

### music_en_026 🚨

- **Question**: "Which woodwind instrument is the largest member of its family commonly used in orchestras and is sometimes called the 'clown of the orchestra'?"
- **Marked correct**: Bassoon
- **Distractor / Clarity issue**: contrabassoon (a distractor) is larger than the bassoon and is used in orchestras (Beethoven 5/9, Brahms, Mahler, R. Strauss all score it). "Largest member of its family commonly used in orchestras" therefore points to contrabassoon by literal reading, even though "clown of the orchestra" still uniquely identifies the bassoon.
- **Suggested fix**: drop the "largest" clause and lean on the nickname — "Which woodwind instrument is sometimes called the 'clown of the orchestra'?"

### music_en_037 🚨

- **Question**: "What is the highest individual Grammy honor, won multiple times by classical conductor Sir Georg Solti?"
- **Marked correct**: "Most Grammy Awards won by an individual"
- **Clarity fail**: "Highest individual Grammy honor" is not a real Grammy category; it's awkwardly describing a *record* (most wins ever). The correct answer is an English phrase, not an award. The distractors are real awards, so they read as more plausible than the right answer.
- **Factual concern**: Beyoncé passed Solti in 2023 (32 Grammys to Solti's 31); the fun fact's "in the 2020s" is correct but the question framing makes Solti sound like the current holder.
- **Suggested fix**: rewrite as a direct trivia question — "Which classical conductor held the record for most Grammy Awards won by an individual until Beyoncé surpassed him in 2023?" with Solti as the answer, distractors being other classical conductors.

### music_en_049 🚨

- **Question**: "Which Italian word marks the slowest standard tempo in classical music?"
- **Marked correct**: Largo
- **Factual fail**: Largo (≈40–60 BPM) is not the slowest standard tempo marking. Grave (≈20–40 BPM) and Larghissimo (even slower) are also standard markings used in classical music — the question's own fun_fact admits this. So the "correct" answer is wrong by the question's own framing.
- **Suggested fix**: rephrase to "Which of these Italian tempo markings is the slowest?" with answers Largo / Allegro / Presto — among those three, Largo is unambiguously the slowest. (Or replace with a different superlative question.)

### music_en_050 ⚠️

- **Question**: 1981 Soft Cell hit covering Gloria Jones' 1964 Northern Soul track.
- **Fun-fact concern**: "43 weeks on the US Billboard Hot 100, a record at the time" — the 43-week chart run is documented; "a record at the time" is a softer claim and depends on which chart record you mean (longest run for a single? for a UK act? for any song?). Defensible but worth a sanity check before a live game.
- **Suggested fix (optional)**: trim to "spent 43 weeks on the US Billboard Hot 100, then a remarkable run for any single".
