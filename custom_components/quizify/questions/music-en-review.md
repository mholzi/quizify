## Detailed notes

### music_en_052 🚨

- **Question**: "Which composer's left hand was crippled by an injury, leading him to commission piano concertos written for the left hand alone?"
- **Marked correct**: Paul Wittgenstein
- **Factual fail #1**: Paul Wittgenstein was a **concert pianist**, not a composer. He commissioned works from composers (Ravel, Prokofiev, Strauss, Britten, Korngold, Hindemith) — he didn't compose them himself. The question's "Which composer" framing makes "Paul Wittgenstein" a wrong answer to its own question.
- **Factual fail #2**: It was his **right arm** that was amputated after a WWI injury on the Eastern Front, not his left hand. His **left hand was the surviving one**, which is precisely why he commissioned **left-hand-only** concertos — the left hand was all he had left to play with. The question's "left hand was crippled" inverts the medical reality.
- **Fun_fact directly contradicts the question**: "Pianist Paul Wittgenstein lost his right arm in World War I and commissioned left-hand concertos…" — the fun_fact is correct on both counts where the question is wrong.
- **Suggested fix**: reframe as "Which Austrian pianist lost his right arm in World War I and commissioned a series of piano concertos written for the left hand alone?" Keep the answer.

### music_en_057 ⚠️

- **Question**: "Which jazz pianist composed 'Round Midnight'…"
- **Marked correct**: Thelonious Monk — correct.
- **Fun_fact concern**: "Monk reportedly began writing 'Round Midnight' as a teenager in the 1930s." Common pop-trivia, but the authoritative source (Robin Kelley, *Thelonious Monk: The Life and Times of an American Original*, 2009) places composition around 1940–44 (Monk was 22–26). Cootie Williams cut the first famous recording in 1944. The teenager-in-the-30s claim should be softened or replaced.
- **Suggested fix**: replace the dubious origin claim with a verifiable fact: "Monk's angular, dissonant style was initially dismissed as 'wrong notes' before being recognised as visionary; 'Round Midnight' is one of the most recorded jazz standards in history."

### music_en_061 ⚠️

- **Question**: "Which Roland drum machine, originally a commercial flop, became the iconic sound of acid house in the late 1980s?"
- **Marked correct**: TR-808
- **Factual concern**: The TR-808 was indeed a commercial flop and became hugely influential second-hand — but its iconic association is with **hip-hop, electro, Miami bass, and R&B** (Marvin Gaye's "Sexual Healing", Afrika Bambaataa's "Planet Rock", essentially all 1980s–2000s rap production). **Acid house's** defining sound is the Roland **TB-303** bass synthesiser (the squelchy resonant filter). The TR-909 is the drum machine more commonly paired with acid house and Detroit techno. The 808 → acid house attribution swaps two distinct pieces of Roland gear.
- **Suggested fix**: change "the iconic sound of acid house" to "an iconic sound of hip-hop and electronic dance music" — preserves the "flop turned legend" narrative without the genre error.

### music_en_079 ⚠️

- **Question**: "Which large pipe-organ component pushes air through the pipes to produce sound?"
- **Marked correct**: Bellows
- **Clarity concern**: Modern pipe organs use electric **blowers/fans**, not bellows. Historically (pre-electric, roughly pre-1900) bellows did this job. The question has no time qualifier so "bellows" is correct in a historical sense and arguably wrong in a modern one. The fun_fact correctly notes "before electric blowers". The question itself needs the historical qualifier.
- **Suggested fix**: rephrase as "Which pipe-organ component historically pumped air through the pipes before electric blowers replaced them?" — same answer, no ambiguity.

### music_en_081 🚨

### music_en_089 🚨

- **Question**: "Which 1990s music technology standard let electronic instruments and computers exchange performance data over a simple cable?"
- **Marked correct**: MIDI
- **Factual fail**: MIDI 1.0 was finalised in **1983**, not the 1990s. The fun_fact correctly states "MIDI 1.0 was finalised in 1983". Question and fun_fact directly contradict each other. MIDI was already ubiquitous by the late 1980s (every DX7, every early DAW, every sequencer).
- **Suggested fix**: change "1990s" → "1980s" in the question.

### music_en_090 ⚠️

- **Question**: "Which Beatles album was the band's first to be recorded entirely on eight-track tape?"
- **Marked correct**: Abbey Road
- **Clarity concern**: Canonical telling: Abbey Road (1969) was the first complete Beatles album recorded on 8-track. Late White Album (1968) sessions and some Get Back / Let It Be sessions also touched 8-track machines as they came online at EMI/Trident. "Entirely on 8-track" is tight but defensible — Sgt Pepper and Revolver were 4-track.
- **Verdict**: keep as-is — distractors clearly wrong, canonical answer holds.

### music_en_097 ⚠️

- **Question**: "Which 1959 American Brill Building songwriting duo wrote pop hits including 'Will You Love Me Tomorrow' and 'The Loco-Motion'?"
- **Marked correct**: Goffin and King
- **Clarity concern**: The "1959" label is imprecise — Goffin and King married in 1959 and signed with Aldon Music that year, but their hit-making years were 1960–1965. The cited songs are 1960 (Will You Love Me Tomorrow / Shirelles) and 1962 (The Loco-Motion / Little Eva). Calling them a "1959 duo" reads like the songs were written in 1959, which is wrong.
- **Suggested fix**: drop "1959" — just say "Which American Brill Building songwriting duo wrote 1960s pop hits including…"
