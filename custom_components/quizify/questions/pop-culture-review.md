### pop_en_053 ⚠️

- **Question**: "What is the asking price for the head of the Hollywood Sign letters that were sold off in the 1980s?"
- **Marked correct**: Each letter was sponsored by a donor for around $27,000.
- **Factual concern**: Sign restoration was 1978, not "1980s." The fun fact itself says 1978 — the question contradicts its own fun fact.
- **Clarity concern**: "Asking price for the head of" is awkward English. "Head of the letters" reads as if there's a "head" person or "head" letter.
- **Suggested fix**: Rephrase to "When the rotting Hollywood Sign was restored in 1978, how much did each sponsor pay for a single letter?" — keeps the same correct answer and removes both issues.

### pop_en_058 🚨

- **Question**: "Which Marvel film's working title was 'Group Hug' to fool fans?"
- **Marked correct**: Avengers: Infinity War.
- **Factual concern (fun fact)**: Public reporting (Joe Russo interviews, leaked clapperboards) attributes "Mary Lou" as the working title used for *Infinity War* — the fun fact's claim that *Endgame* shot under "Mary Lou" appears to be reversed. Additionally, "Group Hug" as *Infinity War*'s decoy title is not the most-cited Marvel working title (commonly cited Marvel decoys include "Mary Lou", "Mary Lou 2", "Aquarius"); this specific attribution should be verified before shipping.
- **Suggested fix**: Either (a) rewrite the question with a verified Marvel working title (e.g., "Mary Lou" → *Infinity War*) and adjust distractors, or (b) delete the question if no clean source can be confirmed. The ambiguity around which fake title belongs to which Marvel film makes this a deletion candidate per pack policy (one bad question taints the round).

### pop_en_061 ⚠️

- **Question**: "Which sitcom finale was watched live by more US viewers than any other scripted episode in history?"
- **Marked correct**: M*A*S*H.
- **Clarity concern**: M*A*S*H is broadly classified as a dramedy (single-camera, no laugh track in many episodes, frequent dramatic-war themes), not a traditional sitcom. The 106M-viewer claim itself is correct for "most-watched scripted episode." Calling M*A*S*H a "sitcom" is the kind of debatable label a knowledgeable player will challenge mid-game.
- **Suggested fix**: Replace "sitcom finale" with "TV series finale" — the record stands the same and the wording is unambiguous.

### pop_en_065 ⚠️

- **Question**: "Which long-running TV show's pilot featured a completely different lead actor before being recast?"
- **Marked correct**: The Big Bang Theory.
- **Factual / clarity concern**: Both Jim Parsons (Sheldon) and Johnny Galecki (Leonard) — the show's two male leads — were in the unaired 2006 pilot. What changed was the female lead (Katie, played by Amanda Walsh, became Penny / Kaley Cuoco) and supporting characters. The current phrasing "completely different lead actor" reads as describing a male-lead recast, which is false.
- **Suggested fix**: Rephrase to "Which long-running sitcom's unaired pilot featured a completely different female lead before being recast?" — same correct answer, but accurate.

### pop_en_106 ⚠️

- **Question**: "Which famous talk-show host kept the same band leader for over 30 years?"
- **Marked correct**: Johnny Carson, with Doc Severinsen.
- **Factual concern**: Severinsen led the NBC Orchestra on *The Tonight Show* from 1967 to 1992 — 25 years, not "over 30." (Carson himself hosted from 1962 to 1992 = 30 years; Severinsen had been a band trumpeter under Skitch Henderson before being promoted to leader.) "Over 30 years" as written is inaccurate.
- **Suggested fix**: Rephrase to "Which famous talk-show host kept the same band leader for 25 years?" — keeps the answer right and makes the duration factual. Distractor durations (Letterman/Shaffer ≈ 33 years across NBC + CBS; Leno/Eubanks ≈ 17 years) remain plausibly distinguishable.
