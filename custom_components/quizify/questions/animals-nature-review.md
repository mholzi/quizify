### nat_en_087 concern

- **Question**: Which animal is estimated to have the most teeth — over 20,000 on a microscopic 'tongue'?
- **Marked correct**: Garden snail
- **Factual concern**: The radula of the common garden snail (*Helix aspersa* / *Cornu aspersum*) is most often cited at around **14,000-15,000 teeth** (e.g. ~14,175 in widely-reproduced figures). Some popular-science sources cite "over 25,000" or "20,000+", but the lower number is more commonly cited in malacology references. The specific quiz claim "over 20,000" is defensible but sits on the high end of the range — a sharp player might know the lower figure and dispute the answer.
- **Distractor note**: Distractors (catfish, sea urchin) are good — catfish do have many teeth but nowhere near 14k+, sea urchins use Aristotle's lantern (5 teeth). Plausible-but-clearly-wrong, fine.
- **Fun-fact**: Solid — Barber et al. 2015 limpet-tooth-strength claim is well-published.
- **Suggested fix** (optional, no auto-fix applied): rephrase question to "Which animal has been estimated to have many thousands of teeth on a microscopic 'tongue'?" — same answer, weaker numeric claim. Or change number to "over 10,000" to safely span the citation range. Not flagged as `fix_needed` because the answer (garden snail) is uniquely correct among the distractors regardless of the exact count.
