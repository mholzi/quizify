## Detailed notes

### history_en_008 ⚠️

- **Question**: What strange object did Roman soldiers receive part of their salary in?
- **Marked correct**: Salt
- **Factual concern**: The "Roman soldiers paid in salt" claim and the salarium-from-salt etymology are widely cited but disputed among modern Latin scholars; "salarium" likely meant a stipend (perhaps originally to buy salt with), not literal salt payment.
- **Suggested fix**: Keep — sufficiently grounded for a fun-trivia question, fun_fact already softens to "salt allowance".

### history_en_017 🚨

- **Question**: What odd item did the Pope ban from chess sets in 1061?
- **Marked correct**: The game itself, calling it a vice
- **Clarity fail**: The phrasing "ban from chess sets" implies banning a component IN a set; the correct answer breaks the question's premise.
- **Suggested fix**: Rewrite question to "What did the Pope condemn as a vice in 1061?" or similar so the correct answer fits naturally.

### history_en_023 🚨

- **Question**: What strange item did pirates almost never actually wear, despite the modern image?
- **Marked correct**: Eye patches over a blind eye
- **Factual concern**: The "dark-adapted eye" claim from the fun_fact is a popular MythBusters-era hypothesis with zero contemporary historical evidence. Presenting it as the real reason for pirate eye patches misleads players.
- **Suggested fix**: Delete — the premise rests on a modern speculation, not history.

### history_en_026 🚨

- **Question**: What did Benjamin Franklin propose as the national bird of the United States?
- **Marked correct**: The wild turkey
- **Factual fail**: Franklin never formally proposed the turkey. His 1784 private letter to his daughter Sarah Bache compared the bald eagle unfavorably to the turkey but did not propose the turkey as a national symbol. This is a textbook American history myth.
- **Suggested fix**: Delete — the question's premise is itself the misconception.

### history_en_030 🚨

- **Question**: What did Thomas Jefferson keep on display at the White House from Lewis and Clark?
- **Marked correct**: Two live grizzly bear cubs
- **Factual fail**: The grizzly cubs at the White House (1807) were a gift from Zebulon Pike's expedition, not Lewis and Clark. The fun_fact correctly attributes them to Pike — internally contradicting the question.
- **Suggested fix**: Rewrite to "from explorer Zebulon Pike" so question and fun_fact align.

### history_en_033 🚨

- **Question**: What did the British actually use to camouflage tanks in World War I?
- **Marked correct**: Painted dazzle patterns inspired by Cubism
- **Factual fail**: Dazzle camouflage was a NAVAL technique developed by Norman Wilkinson for SHIPS, not tanks. WWI tanks had bold patterned paint schemes but not dazzle per se.
- **Suggested fix**: Rewrite question to be about ships, not tanks.

### history_en_034 ⚠️

- **Question**: What weird item did the CIA try to weaponise in the 1960s under Project Acoustic Kitty?
- **Marked correct**: A surgically implanted cat
- **Fun_fact concern**: The "hit by a taxi" outcome was widely reported but disputed by former CIA officer Robert Wallace, who said the cat was simply returned. Project cost estimates also vary.
- **Suggested fix**: Soften fun_fact to "reportedly" — keep.

### history_en_037 ⚠️

- **Question**: What odd item was found in Hitler's personal bunker after the war?
- **Marked correct**: A vegetarian cookbook collection
- **Factual concern**: No well-sourced documentation confirms a "vegetarian cookbook collection" specifically in the Führerbunker. Hitler's vegetarianism is well-documented; the cookbook claim is not.
- **Suggested fix**: Delete — premise unverifiable.

### history_en_049 🚨

- **Question**: What did Soviet engineers seriously propose to defeat the cold during Operation Barbarossa's winter?
- **Marked correct**: Mixing kerosene into engine oil
- **Clarity/factual concern**: Operation Barbarossa was the GERMAN invasion of the USSR. Framing Soviet engineers as acting "during Barbarossa's winter" conflates the perspectives. The kerosene-in-oil trick is real but it's a Soviet response, not a Barbarossa-side action.
- **Suggested fix**: Rephrase to "during the winter of 1941-42" without naming Barbarossa from the Soviet side.
