## Detailed notes

### history_en_054 ⚠️

- **Question**: What unusual currency did the people of Yap Island use for centuries?
- **Marked correct**: "Giant stone wheels up to four metres wide"
- **Factual concern**: The largest documented Rai stone (on Rumung island, near Riy village) is 3.6 m (12 ft) in diameter — not 4 m. Most are between 30 and 50 cm. "Up to four metres" overstates the maximum by ~10%.
- **Suggested fix**: change "up to four metres wide" → "over three metres wide" or "more than three metres wide".

### history_en_055 ⚠️

- **Question**: What did medieval European bakers fear losing if they were caught short-weighting bread?
- **Marked correct**: "A hand or an ear"
- **Factual concern**: Under the 1266 Assize of Bread and Ale (Henry III), the documented punishments for short-weighting were fines, the pillory, beatings/flogging, and imprisonment. The "judicial amputation of a hand" or "ear nailed to the wall" story circulates in some popular accounts but is widely flagged as folklore — Wikipedia's talk page calls it "typical medieval folklore (more likely with origins post-Enlightenment)." None of the three answer options cleanly matches the mainstream historical record.
- **Suggested fix**: Either delete (cleaner) or replace correct answer with something like "Time in the pillory" and rotate distractors.

### history_en_057 🚨

- **Question**: What did Vikings actually wear on their heads in battle?
- **Marked correct**: "Simple iron or leather caps with no horns"
- **Factual**: question itself is fine — Vikings didn't wear horned helmets.
- **Fun-fact fail**: "Archaeology has produced exactly one largely intact Viking helmet — the Gjermundbu — and it has no horns." This is wrong. The Yarm helmet (Yorkshire, found 1950s, authenticated 2020 by Chris Caple via metallurgical analysis) is a second near-complete Viking-age helmet — the only one ever found in Britain. The Smithsonian, IFLScience, and Wikipedia all describe Gjermundbu and Yarm as the two near-complete Viking helmets known to archaeology.
- **Suggested fix**: change fun fact's "exactly one" → "only two" (and optionally mention both Gjermundbu and Yarm).

### history_en_087 ⚠️

- **Question**: What unusual practice did the Hittites use in diplomacy around 1300 BC?
- **Marked correct**: "Signed one of history's earliest written peace treaties"
- **Factual concern**: The Egyptian–Hittite peace treaty (Treaty of Kadesh) is dated ~1259/1258 BC, signed between Ramesses II and Ḫattušili III. "Around 1300 BC" is ~40 years too early.
- **Suggested fix**: change "around 1300 BC" → "around 1259 BC" (or "in the 13th century BC").

### history_en_101 ⚠️

- **Question**: What surprising thing did Pope Gregory IX issue a papal bull against in 1233?
- **Marked correct**: "Cats, especially black ones"
- **Factual concern**: Vox in Rama (June 1233) condemned the heresy of Luciferianism in Germany. It described a satanic cult ritual involving a statue of a black cat. The bull does not directly declare cats themselves heretical, nor advocate killing them — see Wikipedia/Snopes. The "Pope banned cats" framing is popular but technically misleading. The causal chain to the Black Death via mass cat killing is also disputed by historians.
- **Suggested fix**: Either accept as concern (popular-history framing) and leave; OR rewrite to specify the satanic cult — but that's harder for a quiz card. Recommendation: keep as-is with concern flag, since the trivia framing is widely repeated; mark for Markus' judgment.

### history_en_102 ⚠️

- **Question**: What did the Aztecs send the Spanish in 1519 as a diplomatic offering?
- **Marked correct**: "Live turkeys, cacao and gold ornaments"
- **Factual concern**: Spanish chroniclers document specific 1519 gifts as including: an Aztec calendar (gold sun-disc, silver moon-disc), a necklace of golden crabs and other fine gold jewellery, feathered cloaks, and chocolate/cacao. Live turkeys, while valued in Aztec tribute generally, are not the most-cited specific gift at Cortés first-contact. The phrasing is loose rather than wrong, but it weakens the "factually correct" criterion.
- **Suggested fix**: change "Live turkeys, cacao and gold ornaments" → "Gold ornaments, feathered cloaks and cacao".
