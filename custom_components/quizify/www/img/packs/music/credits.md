## Why none of this is recorded music

Music is one of the two subjects #554 flagged as a licence wall, and it is real:
sleeve art, publicity photographs and concert pictures are effectively never
freely licensed. What *is* free is everything upstream of the recording
industry — instruments in museum collections, composer portraits out of
copyright, manuscripts, and the machine that started playback. The pack keeps
its modern questions in text; the pictures live before 1900.

## Dropped, and why

* **`Harpsichord MET DP227669.jpg`** — CC0 and 3893 px, so it passed both usual
  tests, and it is still unusable: the photograph shows the painted underside
  of the lid alone. At card size it reads as a painting of sea nymphs, not as
  an instrument, and the question asks which keyboard instrument this is.
  Replaced with the Met's older full view, which shows the two keyboards.

## Sizes

Up to 1100 px on the long edge; the violin, the manuscript and the harpsichord
keep their native sizes (941–1048 px). WebP at quality 80, 26–193 KB each,
458 KB for the folder. Each was rendered at 340 px — the width the card gets on
a 390 px phone — and looked at before being kept. The manuscript is the
smallest source of the set at 917 px and the one that matters least: it has to
read as handwritten notation, not as individual notes.
