## v1.8.0 — Play as a Team, and a Picture That Comes Into Focus

One addition here changes who is playing. Until now Quizify counted people; from this release it can count sofas. Two people who want to play together say so on their phones, and from that point the room has one fewer participant and one more team.

The second addition is smaller and only shows up on picture rounds: the photograph now opens blurred and sharpens while the clock drains, so recognising it early is worth something.

### 👥 Team mode

Players form teams themselves, in the lobby, before the first question ([#365](https://github.com/mholzi/quizify/issues/365)). The host assigns nobody. The lobby asks one question — playing alone, or with someone? — and only a player who answers "with someone" ever sees a team list. Naming a team is optional; leave the field empty and one gets suggested.

**A team is a participant, not a grouping.** It answers once, scores once, and appears in the ranking exactly where a player would. There is no per-capita division and no handicap: four people on one sofa may out-score a pair, and that is the mode working as intended rather than a fairness bug. A player who joins no team keeps their own row next to the teams — a lone player is a team of one, not an error state.

**Any member may change the team's answer until the clock stops.** The answer standing at the buzzer is the team's answer, and the speed bonus keys on the *last* tap, so tapping something instantly and thinking afterwards is not free. A short lock after each change stops two people flipping the answer back and forth in the final seconds. This is deliberately scoped to team mode: a solo player's answer is still final the moment they tap it.

On the question screen the standing answer shows as small member dots on the answer it sits on — no banner. Disagreement shows as dots on two different rows. The cost is accepted: it is quiet, and someone mid-tap notices another member's change only if they look.

**The end-of-game awards go to teams.** Not as a rename — a team records what a player records, so each award gets a reading that follows from the data. Fastest Finger is the time of the tap that *stood*, which means a team that argues to the buzzer genuinely is slower. Buzzkill sums the freezes its members spent, since power-ups are still handed to people.

The Lightning Round works the same way ([#552](https://github.com/mholzi/quizify/issues/552)): one answer, one score, changeable until the clock stops. Two consequences follow from that rather than from taste — the lightning question now runs its full window in team mode instead of ending when everyone has answered, and the recap names the team.

The television shows the lobby grouped by team, so the room can see who is playing with whom without anyone shouting across the sofa.

### 🖼️ A picture that comes into focus

An image question may declare `reveal_style: "progressive"` ([#434](https://github.com/mholzi/quizify/issues/434)). The picture opens heavily blurred and sharpens as the round timer drains, which lines up the reveal with the speed bonus the scoring already pays. It rides the countdown both clients already receive, so the mechanic needs no message of its own. Enabled on the two picture packs; every other pack behaves exactly as before.

Three silent failure modes are guarded, because each one would have left a green suite and a broken round: the reveal reuses the question view's image and has to clear the blur explicitly, the player's phone and the zoom overlay hold their own copies that must blur too, and a style declared without an image is dropped at parse time. A typo'd `"progresive"` falls back to showing the picture rather than leaving it permanently unreadable. There is deliberately no reduced-motion override — clearing the blur there would hand the answer to whoever asked for calmer motion.

### ✨ Polish

Three things a full browser pass turned up ([#548](https://github.com/mholzi/quizify/pull/548)). The shareable result card printed the pack *slug* — it is written to be pasted into a group chat, where "picture-round-en" reads like a filename. The four packs that ship `theme: trivia` had no icon of their own and fell through to the glyph that means "every pack at once". And the image zoom button was anchored to the media box rather than to the picture, so on a portrait image it floated in empty space.

### 🙏 Thank you

Team mode was shaped by playing it on three phones and a television, not by re-reading the diff — several things only showed up at the table. If your evening finds something odd — a round that ends too early, a screen that disagrees with the one next to it — that report is worth more than another pass over the code.
